1 · What this project actually is

One-liner: A web app that scores a student's resume against a target role, shows exactly what's missing with free Indian gov/edu resources to fix it, then matches them to jobs they can credibly apply to today.

Stack (locked at AGENTS.md):

  • Vite + React 19 — single-page, no router (5 tabs via useState('view') in app/src/App.jsx:16)
  • Tailwind v4 via @tailwindcss/vite (already dark-themed — bg #0a0a0f, accent violet #a78bfa)
  • pdfjs-dist for in-browser PDF parse — privacy-critical, file never uploaded
  • @google/generative-ai (Gemini Flash) — optional, app works offline without it via localAdapter
  • Supabase — listed but not wired in Build 1; planned for Part 3 (auth + score persistence)

The 3-minute demo loop (PRD.md §8):

  1. 0:00 — Pick role (SDE) → Try sample resume → ATS ~55
  2. 0:40 — Show breakdown bars → "here's why 55"
  3. 1:10 — Show gaps → click free NPTEL/Striver link → "zero friction"
  4. 1:40 — Jobs tab → 1 eligible Apply, 1 locked "need 15 pts"
  5. 2:10 — Save score → college avg moves
  6. 2:30 — Mock interview 1 Q + Grade
  7. 2:50 — Privacy line + offline line + "AI unlocks with key"

2 · How the scoring works (the part judges will probe)

The rubric is in app/src/lib/score.js and is transparent on purpose — the score must be defensible, not a black box.

BucketMaxHow it's measured
Skills30Intersection of role's required skills with resume tokens
Keywords20Role-specific keyword density
Experience25Internship / project / lead indicators
Format15Section headers, bullet count, length sanity
Impact10Numbers / metrics present in bullets

Key design choices and why:

  • Cap at 95, never 100. Leaves headroom for the re-upload loop — the score can always go up, which is what makes the gamification loop work (PRD.md §5).
  • Fixed 4 roles (sde / data / marketing / govt). Adding a role = one entry per data file (PRD.md §5). Don't add roles mid-hackathon — scope creep.
  • Missing-skills list, not "score breakdown" is the primary CTA. The "Fix next" card on the Jobs page (App.jsx:131-141) shows the top 2 missing skills with a direct free course link. This is the **value moment**.

3 · Privacy & offline — the two trust claims

Trust Claim #1 — Privacy

"Your PDF is parsed in your browser. Only the text + score are stored, never the file." Implementation: app/src/lib/parseResume.js uses pdfjs-dist running in a Web Worker, and App.jsx:48 immediately clears e.target.value after read.

Trust Claim #2 — Offline-first

The local rubric + curated jobs + 12 courses work without network. But the pdfjs worker is loaded from unpkg.com CDN — if stage Wi-Fi fails on first PDF upload, the claim breaks. Fix before submission: vendor the worker file locally. (See §6 Priority 1.)

4 · Likely judge questions (with model answers)

These are the questions a govt-hackathon panel will actually ask, ranked by frequency. Each has a 2-sentence spoken answer you can memorise.

Q1 How is this different from Naukri / LinkedIn / Internshala?

Answer: Those are job boards — they list everything. We start from your resume and only show jobs you're actually eligible for, with the exact free course to unlock the locked ones. One-loop, not browse-and-pray.

Q2 How do you know your ATS score is accurate?

Answer: The score is fully transparent — five buckets with max points each, shown as bars. It uses a deterministic keyword rubric that matches the same skills we use to filter jobs, so the score is the eligibility logic. Gemini can enrich it, but the local rubric is the source of truth (App.jsx:169-178, PRD.md §5).

Q3 What happens to student data? Are you uploading resumes?

Answer: No. PDFs are parsed in your browser via WebAssembly and the file handle is cleared immediately (App.jsx:48). Only the extracted text and the score live in memory. Supabase will only persist the score, not the resume, with explicit consent in Part 3.

Q4 Does this work without internet?

Answer: Yes — the score, jobs, courses, mock interview, and college battle all run on local curated data. Only the AI rewrite and AI interview grading need a key, and they have a local fallback that still gives useful tips.

Q5 What's your scale plan? How many students can this serve?

Answer: Static Vite build + Supabase free tier covers ~50K MAU easily. All hot data (jobs, courses, role rubrics) is read-only and CDN-cacheable. Only score writes hit the DB, which is a small payload.

Q6 How will Tier-2/3 students actually find this?

Answer: College partnerships via the placement cell — one principal sign-off lets us onboard a whole college's graduating batch. We ship a college-battle mechanic that gives the cell a free "average placement readiness" dashboard, which is their actual KPI.

Q7 Why is the cap 95 and not 100?

Answer: Intentional headroom for the re-upload loop. If a student hits 100, the score stops being a goal. 95 means every fix can move the number, which is what makes the gamification work.

Q8 How do you prevent fake scores / gaming?

Answer: The score is recomputed from the resume text every time — there's no input field for "score". To inflate it you'd need to actually add the missing skills to your resume, at which point… you've improved your resume. That's the point.

Q9 What about AI bias? Gemini might rate some resumes unfairly.

Answer: AI is opt-in and only enriches, never decides. The eligibility gate (locked vs unlocked jobs) runs on the local rubric. If the AI fails or is unfair, the student still gets the deterministic score and free course links.

Q10 Why these four roles and not, say, design or finance?

Answer: We picked the four with the highest free-resource density in India: SDE (freeCodeCamp/Striver), Data (NPTEL), Marketing (Google Skillshop), Govt (NPTEL + SSC/UPSC). Adding a role is a one-file change per data module — we can expand post-launch.

Q11 What did you cut from scope and why?

Answer: Voice/video interview analysis, live job scraping, and a public resume leaderboard. Voice needs ML we can't ship in the window; live scraping is a TOS nightmare; public ranking creates PII and demotivation risk (PRD.md §7, Q3 in grill session).

Q12 How is this different from a ChatGPT wrapper?

Answer: The score, jobs, courses, and rubric are all deterministic and local. Gemini is one of three optional enrichments, not the product. The product is the closed loop — score → gap → fix → re-score → apply — which a chatbot can't do because it has no state and no curated Indian resource map.

Q13 What's your monetisation path?

Answer: Free for students, always. Revenue from B2B: college placement cells pay for a dashboard (anonymised cohort readiness), and partner course providers (NPTEL-affiliated) pay for featured placement in the gap-fixer. No ads, no student data sales.

Q14 What if a student pastes a 10-page resume?

Answer: PDF parse is capped at first 3 pages (AGENTS.md). Pasting 10 pages works but the score is still deterministic — long resumes don't artificially inflate it; they actually hurt the Format bucket if bullets are missing.

Q15 What if NPTEL links rot?

Answer: All links are versioned in app/src/data/courses.js. A nightly check script (Part 3) flags 4xx and queues replacements. The whole courses map is one file, so a fix is one PR.

Q16 How do you handle regional languages?

Answer: Out of scope for Build 1. The Hindi-belt colleges we surveyed write resumes in English even when their medium of instruction is Hindi, so the English rubric is sufficient for v1. Localisation is on the Part 3 roadmap.

Q17 Who owns the data if a college wants to export?

Answer: Students own their data. Colleges see anonymised cohort aggregates only, with explicit opt-in at upload time. Export = student downloads their own JSON.

5 · What to add (ranked recommendations)

Ordered by impact per hour of work. P0 = must do before submission. P3 = nice-to-have if you have a free evening.

P0 Vendor the pdfjs worker locally

Why: Your privacy + offline claim depends on it. Currently unpkg.com is in the hot path. One file move + a Vite asset import. Time: 1 hour. Test: turn off Wi-Fi, upload a PDF, verify it still parses.

P0 Add a "demo mode" URL flag

Why: Judges will arrive mid-conversation. ?demo=1 should pre-load sample resume, jump to SDE, score already visible. Saves you 30 seconds and removes human error from the demo. Time: 2 hours.

P0 Ship a one-pager PDF for judges who won't run the demo

Why: Govt panels often read asynchronously. Include the screenshot, the rubric, the privacy line, and the 3 demo links. Time: 3 hours. Use the same dark theme.

P0 Verify on the MP Online portal submission checklist

Why: Read the actual submission rules — file format, video required, slide deck limit. Many govt hackathons require a 2-min video walkthrough. Don't discover this the day before. Time: 1 hour to read + screenshot the rules.

P1 Add 3 more curated jobs per role (12 → 24)

Why: The Jobs tab is what judges screenshot. 12 feels thin. Add verified Internshala/Naukri apply URLs only — no broken links. Time: 4 hours.

P1 Add a "Why this score?" tooltip on each bar

Why: The bars already work, but a hover tooltip showing "Skills 24/30 — you have React, missing: TypeScript, Redis" makes the rubric unforgeable. Time: 3 hours.

P1 Wire the Supabase score-save (Part 3 lite)

Why: Even without auth, an anonymous localStorage-backed history view ("your last 3 scores: 55 → 62 → 71") makes the re-upload loop visible. Judge can see the gamification working. Time: 4 hours.

P1 Add a Hindi landing line + 1-line mission statement

Why: MP judges respond to "Tier-2/3 student" framing in the local language. Add one Hindi sentence to the header. Don't translate the whole app — just the value prop. Time: 1 hour.

P2 Add 2 more roles (Design, Finance)

Why: Easy wins because of the data-module pattern. But don't ship untested roles — each role needs ≥3 verified jobs. Time: 6 hours per role.

P2 Add an "interview STAR grader" local heuristic

Why: Currently the local mock-interview grader just counts answers >10 chars. A simple "look for numbers, look for 'I' vs 'we'" check makes the offline grading meaningful without Gemini. Time: 3 hours.

P2 Add PWA support (install-to-home-screen)

Why: Tier-2/3 students are on low-end Android with spotty Wi-Fi. A PWA shell with cached assets makes the offline claim real, not aspirational. Time: 4 hours. Use vite-plugin-pwa.

P2 Record a 2-min fallback demo video

Why: If the live demo fails (and it will, eventually), you switch to the video without losing face. Embed it on a hidden route or keep the file ready. Time: 4 hours.

P3 Add cohort-ready analytics for college placement cells

Why: This is your B2B story. Even a static screenshot of "what the dashboard would look like" sells the monetisation path. Time: 6 hours for a mock.

P3 Add a referral / share-score mechanic

Why: Viral loop for college spread. "Challenge a friend from another college" with anonymised scores. Time: 8 hours.

P3 Voice-input mock interview (Web Speech API)

Why: Differentiator for judges who've seen 50 text chatbots. But adds complexity — only if P0/P1/P2 are done. Time: 8 hours.

Things to NOT add before submission
  • Real-time multi-user leaderboard — privacy and infra headache, kill per PRD §7
  • Voice/video interview analysis — out of scope by design (PRD.md §7)
  • Live Naukri/LinkedIn scraping — TOS risk + flaky
  • Custom design system / Tailwind overhaul — ship the dark theme you have
  • Full i18n — one Hindi sentence is enough; full translation is a rabbit hole

6 · The 60-second pitch (memorise this)

"Every year, lakhs of Tier-2 and Tier-3 Indian students graduate without knowing what skills their target companies actually want. Campus2Corporate closes that loop in 3 minutes: drop in your resume, get a transparent ATS score, see the top 3 skills you're missing with free NPTEL or YouTube links to fix them today, and see only the jobs you're actually eligible for. Your PDF never leaves your browser. It works offline on stage Wi-Fi. The score is open — every point is auditable. And the AI rewrite is optional, not the gatekeeper. We're not a chatbot; we're a deterministic loop that turns a confused student into a credible applicant by Friday."

Word count: 124. Spoken at 130 wpm = ~57 seconds. Leaves room for the demo to start.
